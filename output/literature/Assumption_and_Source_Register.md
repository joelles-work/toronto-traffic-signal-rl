# Assumption and Source Register

November 13, 2026

Every row cites its origin or defining protocol. Published precedent, software defaults, site guidance and our own declared choices are different kinds of support: a citation does not establish that an exact value is optimal.

E1 is the frozen pilot protocol; E2 is the completed 25-case dev validation; E3 records the conditional timing checks and proposed training-derived bounds. These are primary records of our own study, not independent peer-reviewed validation.

## Training settings

| Assumption / choice | Actual value | Source and what it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | Two hidden layers of 64 per network/head. | Small CPU-friendly preset. SB3 defaults provide software precedent; a 64/64 encoder also appears in Toronto work [P8](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772586325000048). DQN uses a Q/target pair, while PPO uses policy/value networks, so identical widths do not mean identical models. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Learning rate | 0.0003 for DQN and PPO. | PPO default and used in PedSLight [D2](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html), [P3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2026.1953597/full). It is above SB3 DQN's 0.0001 default [D3](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/dqn.html). A shared rate is a reproducible preset, not evidence of fair or optimal tuning for both algorithms. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Discount | 0.99 for both. | Precedent in pedestrian-aware DQN and PPO [P1](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/11/21/3519), [P3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2026.1953597/full). Other studies use 0.75 or 0.8 [P2](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/itr2.12300), [P5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11634). Because our decisions span 120 s, matching a discount used with one-second actions does not match its time horizon. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Batch size | 64. | Direct precedent in two pedestrian-aware studies [P1](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/11/21/3519), [P2](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/itr2.12300); a manageable CPU minibatch. Other credible work uses 50 or 256 [P3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2026.1953597/full), [P8](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772586325000048), so 64 remains a sensible starting value, not an established optimum. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Total learning | 2,048 decisions per model; three training seeds per algorithm. | Bounded check-in budget, not a convergence justification. Close studies train much longer [P3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2026.1953597/full), [P4](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f0935e4cd5920aa6c7c996a5ee53a70f-Paper-round1.pdf). Equal numbers of decisions control sample budget but not algorithm-specific learning effort or sample efficiency. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| DQN replay | Capacity 20,000. | Exact precedent in Han et al. [P1](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/11/21/3519). Our entire 2,048-transition pilot fits below this capacity, so memory size is not evidence of a fully exercised long-run replay regime. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| DQN start/update | Start 180; one gradient update per new decision. | Collect initial examples before updating, then learn frequently [D3](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/dqn.html). The exact threshold and update ratio are presets; no inspected traffic paper justifies 180 specifically. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| DQN target copy | Every 256 decisions. | Target-network stabilization follows DQN [D3](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/dqn.html), but the period is not its library default and has no exact support in the inspected close studies. Check on dev if longer training is undertaken. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| DQN exploration | Initial epsilon 1.0; decay fraction 0.6; final epsilon 0.05. | Initial/final probabilities have SB3 default precedent [D3](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/dqn.html); stretching decay over 60% is a pilot adaptation. It leaves 5% random actions during late training; deterministic evaluation removes that exploratory randomness. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| PPO collection | 256 decisions per rollout. | Eight rollout-and-update blocks fit into 2,048 decisions. This is a short-budget preset, below the library default 2,048 and PedSLight's 1,000 [D2](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html), [P3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2026.1953597/full); no convergence claim follows from eight updates. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| PPO epochs/GAE | Ten epochs; GAE 0.95. | Both match SB3 defaults and a close pedestrian PPO study [D2](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html), [P3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2026.1953597/full). They control data reuse and reward-estimate smoothing; local optimality would still require dev comparisons. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| PPO entropy | 0.01. | An explicit exploration preset above SB3's zero default [D2](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html). It encourages diverse actions; the reviewed sources do not validate 0.01 for our normalized reward and small budget. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Inherited defaults | PPO clip 0.2, value weight 0.5, gradient cap 0.5; DQN gradient cap 10; Adam; algorithm-specific activations. | Verified from saved models and the installed SB3 implementation [D2](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html), [D3](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/dqn.html). ## Evaluation and seed choices

| Assumption / choice | Actual value | Source and what it supports |

Adjacent hours from one day provide limited distributional coverage; papers do not validate this exact split. A future one-test/more-dev revision should be declared before using the newly assigned periods. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Five demand seeds | 11, 22, 33, 44, 55 for every controller. | Five-run simulation evaluation has benchmark precedent [P4](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f0935e4cd5920aa6c7c996a5ee53a70f-Paper-round1.pdf); common patterns permit paired comparisons. These particular identifiers were fixed in advance [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json), and repeated arrivals within one hour are not independent observed days. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Run structure | Two-hour training, one-hour dev; empty start; no warm-up; up to one-hour drain. | Time windows follow the saved count periods; drain avoids censoring. No warm-up is a limitation for field interpretation. FHWA's example uses lead-in and post-peak simulation [D9](https://highways.dot.gov/media/88726), not a universal fixed warm-up length. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Delay metric | Vehicle timeLoss + departDelay; pedestrian walk timeLoss reported separately. | Grounded in SUMO output semantics [D5](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Output/TripInfo.html). Entry waiting prevents boundary queues disappearing from the score. These are not the same quantities as every paper's average stopped wait, travel time or person-weighted delay. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Completion guard | All generated demand must finish; collisions/teleports invalidate ranking. | Strong protection against censoring bias [P6](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3173/1.pdf). Current 35 cases meet it. Record emergency braking too, but collision-free simulation is not proof of pedestrian safety or low near-conflict risk [P3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2026.1953597/full). Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| Uncertainty | Mean/sample SD; three policy seeds x five traffic patterns; 4,000 paired crossed resamples. | Reporting intervals has methodological support [P10](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f514cec81cb148559cf475e7426eed5e-Paper.pdf). Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |
| What results establish | Fixed/DQN 156.13 s/vehicle; PPO 178.95; all are dev results. | DQN reproduced the baseline on these cases; PPO's interval includes benefit and harm. A dev-selected constant 32/7 comparison now gives 142.97 s/vehicle and 46.98 s/person, providing a stronger simple reference [E2](https://forge.coreweave.com/wandb/joelle1-york-university/toronto-tcs379/runs/wcirpdsc). It is not an independently tested out-of-sample result. Actual implementation/value: [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json). |

## Paper-ready seed statement

Three independently seeded training repetitions (101, 202 and 303) were used to assess training variability, following recommendations for reproducible RL evaluation [P9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560). Five common simulation seeds (11, 22, 33, 44 and 55) generated paired traffic patterns for all controllers, consistent with five-run traffic benchmarking [P4](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f0935e4cd5920aa6c7c996a5ee53a70f-Paper-round1.pdf). The seed identifiers were fixed before training [E1](https://github.com/joelles-work/toronto-traffic-signal-rl/blob/master/experiments/freeflow_pilot_v1/experiment.json); their numeric values carry no claimed statistical advantage.

**D2. Stable-Baselines3.** [PPO documentation](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html). Version 2.9.2, checked November 12, 2026. Parameter defaults, MlpPolicy and supported action spaces; checked against the installed saved model.

**D3. Stable-Baselines3.** [DQN documentation](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/dqn.html). Version 2.9.2, checked November 12, 2026. Parameter defaults, vanilla DQN and discrete action support; checked against the installed saved model.

**D4. Eclipse SUMO.** [Persons specification](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Specification/Persons.html). Checked November 12, 2026. Person-type desired maximum speed and walking model.

**D5. Eclipse SUMO.** [TripInfo output](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Output/TripInfo.html). Checked November 12, 2026. timeLoss, departDelay, unfinished-trip handling and person walk outputs.

**D6. City of Toronto.** [Traffic volumes - multimodal intersection turning movement counts](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/traffic-volumes-at-intersections-for-all-modes/). Toronto Open Data Portal; frozen 2024 rows used in the pilot. Local frozen data, row indices and source dictionary audited; not a research paper.

**D7. City of Toronto.** [Current Signal Timing Information: TCS 379](https://320mccowan.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/TransportationReport_320McCowan.pdf). October 31, 2019, reproduced in WSP transportation report, February 2020. PDF page 147; local phase and runtime validation records. Site-specific source, not a universal timing standard.

**D8. TorontoSUMONetworks contributors.** [TorontoSUMONetworks](https://github.com/Jahandad-Baloch/TorontoSUMONetworks). GitHub revision 35dee7bf8c9963c2105c17befb673be9a9ce597d. Downloaded source and README; starting software reference, not a peer-reviewed validation of our implementation.

**D9. K. Wunderlich, M. Vasudevan and P. Wang / FHWA (2019).** [Traffic Analysis Toolbox Volume III: Guidelines for Applying Traffic Microsimulation Modeling Software](https://highways.dot.gov/media/88726). 2019 update, FHWA-HOP-18-036. Chapters 2, 5 and 6; Appendix A printed page 91 uses a warm-up and post-peak completion period. The example duration is not a universal prescription.

**D10. City of Toronto (2019).** [Pedestrian Timing at Signalised Intersections](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/945f-TS_Traffic-Management_Pedestrian-Timing-Guidelines-2020.pdf), version 2.0, May 6, 2019; Sections 2.1-2.2 and 4.7. Type A is a usual-conditions design assumption; Types B/C and site requirements can require longer WALK times.
